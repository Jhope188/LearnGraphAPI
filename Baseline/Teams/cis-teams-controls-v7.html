8.1 — Teams Settings

TAC → Teams → Teams settings
8.1.1 L2 External file sharing in Teams enabled only for approved cloud storage ▼

Restrict which cloud storage services (Dropbox, Box, Google Drive, Citrix Files, Egnyte) can be attached to Teams channels. Only allow approved providers.

Unrestricted cloud storage integrations allow data exfiltration to unmanaged third-party services outside DLP policy coverage.

TAC → Teams → Teams settings → Files

Medium — users relying on Dropbox/Box/Google Drive integrations will lose them. Audit usage before disabling. User Impact: Medium

8.1.2 L1 Users can't send emails to a channel email address ▼

Disable the ability for users to send emails directly to Teams channel email addresses.

Channel email addresses can be used to inject phishing content, malware, or spam directly into Teams channels, bypassing mail flow rules and Defender for Office 365 controls.

TAC → Teams → Teams settings → Email integration → "Users can send emails to a channel email address" → Off

The screenshot in the original question shows this is currently On — this should be disabled to meet CIS 8.1.2. User Impact: Low

8.2 — Users / External Access

TAC → Users → External access
8.2.1 L2 External domains restricted in Teams admin center ▼

Configure a domain allowlist ("Allow only specific external domains") rather than allowing all external domains. Only trusted partner domains should be permitted.

Allowing all external domains enables any M365 tenant to initiate chat with your users — a common vector for IT support impersonation scams and phishing via Teams.

⚠️ Currently set to "Allow all external domains" — does not meet L2. Acceptable for L1 baseline only.

# Check current state Get-CsTenantFederationConfiguration | Select AllowFederatedUsers, AllowedDomains # Set allowlist (replace with actual domains) Set-CsTenantFederationConfiguration -AllowedDomains @('contoso.com','partner.com')
8.2.2 L1 Communication with unmanaged Teams users is disabled ▼

Disable the "People in my org can chat and have meetings with external users who have unmanaged Microsoft accounts" toggle.

Unmanaged/consumer Teams accounts (personal Microsoft accounts, Teams Free) are not subject to enterprise governance, MFA requirements, or DLP policies.

✅ Currently Off — meets CIS 8.2.2 L1.

Set-CsTenantFederationConfiguration -AllowTeamsConsumer $False
8.2.3 L1 External Teams users cannot initiate conversations ▼

Ensure the sub-option "External users with Teams accounts not managed by an organization can contact users in my organization" is also disabled.

Even if 8.2.2 is met (org cannot reach out), if inbound initiation is allowed, external personal accounts can still contact your users unsolicited. This is a stop-gap when 8.2.2 alone isn't sufficient.

CIS notes this recommendation is superseded if 8.2.2 is fully met. However, confirm both settings are Off to avoid any edge cases. User Impact: Low

Set-CsTenantFederationConfiguration -AllowTeamsConsumerInbound $False
8.2.4 L1 Organization cannot communicate with trial Teams tenants ▼

Disable "People in my organization can communicate with accounts in trial Teams tenant."

Trial tenants have no purchased licenses, minimal governance controls, and are commonly used by threat actors for reconnaissance and phishing campaigns.

✅ Currently Off — meets CIS 8.2.4 L1.

Set-CsTenantFederationConfiguration -ExternalAccessWithTrialTenants "Blocked"

8.5 — Meetings

TAC → Meetings → Meeting policies / Meeting settings
8.5.1 L2 Anonymous users can't join a meeting ▼

Disable "Anonymous users can join a meeting" in Meeting settings.

Anonymous join allows completely unauthenticated participants. Disabling forces all participants to authenticate with a Microsoft identity before joining.

TAC → Meetings → Meeting settings → Participants → Anonymous users can join a meeting → Off

High for customer-facing orgs. External people without a Microsoft account cannot join at all — they must have a Teams or M365 identity. Consider carefully before enforcing. User Impact: High

8.5.2 L1 Anonymous users and dial-in callers can't start a meeting ▼

Ensure anonymous participants and dial-in PSTN callers cannot start a meeting before the organizer joins.

Without this control, anonymous users could join and effectively run a meeting without any authenticated organizer present — enabling eavesdropping or social engineering of other attendees.

TAC → Meetings → Meeting settings → Participants → Anonymous users can start a meeting → Off User Impact: Low
8.5.3 L1 Only people in my org can bypass the lobby ▼

Set "Who can bypass the lobby" to "People in my organization and guests" or more restrictive. External federated users should be held in the lobby.

The lobby is a gatekeeping control. Bypassing it for all external users removes the organizer's ability to vet who has joined before the meeting begins.

TAC → Meetings → Meeting policies → [Policy] → Meeting join & lobby → Who can bypass the lobby User Impact: Medium
8.5.4 L1 Users dialing in can't bypass the lobby ▼

Ensure PSTN dial-in participants are held in the lobby rather than joining directly.

Dial-in PSTN callers have no identity verification beyond knowing the meeting number. Holding them in lobby ensures the organizer admits only expected callers.

TAC → Meetings → Meeting policies → [Policy] → Meeting join & lobby → Dial-in users can bypass the lobby → Off User Impact: Low
8.5.5 L2 Meeting chat does not allow anonymous users ▼

Prevent anonymous meeting participants from posting in meeting chat.

Anonymous users posting in chat could distribute malicious links, impersonate legitimate participants, or exfiltrate information shared in chat during the meeting.

TAC → Meetings → Meeting policies → [Policy] → Meeting engagement → Meeting chat → On for everyone but anonymous users User Impact: Low
8.5.6 L2 Only organizers and co-organizers can present ▼

Restrict the presenter role so only organizers and co-organizers can share screens or present content by default.

Unrestricted presenter access allows external or anonymous users to share screens containing malicious content, disrupt meetings, or conduct visual phishing attacks.

TAC → Meetings → Meeting policies → [Policy] → Content sharing → Who can present → Organizers and co-organizers User Impact: Medium
8.5.7 L1 External participants can't give or request remote control ▼

Disable the ability for external participants to give or request remote control of a presenter's screen.

Remote control is a high-risk capability — an external participant with remote control can execute actions on the presenter's device, install software, or access files. A critical control for preventing IT support scams.

TAC → Meetings → Meeting policies → [Policy] → Content sharing → External participants can give or request control → Off User Impact: Low
8.5.8 L2 External meeting chat is off ▼

Disable chat between internal and external users during meetings.

Meeting chat persists after the meeting ends and can be forwarded externally. Disabling it for external participants reduces the risk of data leakage via chat.

TAC → Meetings → Meeting policies → [Policy] → Meeting engagement → Meeting chat → Off for external users User Impact: Medium
8.5.9 L2 Meeting recording is off by default ▼

Disable cloud recording by default. Recording should be explicitly enabled by policy for roles that require it.

Recordings capture sensitive conversations and are stored in OneDrive/SharePoint where they can be accessed, shared, or breached. Default-off reduces accidental recording of confidential discussions.

TAC → Meetings → Meeting policies → [Policy] → Recording & transcription → Cloud recording → Off User Impact: Medium

8.6 — Messaging

TAC → Messaging → Messaging policies
8.6.1 L1 Users can report security concerns in Teams ▼

Enable the "Report a security concern" feature in Teams so users can flag suspicious messages to the security team or Microsoft.

Teams is increasingly used for phishing and social engineering. Giving users a native reporting mechanism improves threat detection and trains users to identify malicious messages.

TAC → Messaging → Messaging policies → [Policy] → Report a security concern → On User Impact: Low

Cross-Referenced Teams Controls

Defender, Purview & Identity sections
Note: These controls are defined in other CIS sections but directly apply to Teams security posture. They should be reviewed alongside Section 8.
2.1.1 L2 Safe Links enabled for Office Applications (includes Teams) ▼

Enable Safe Links policies to scan and rewrite URLs in Teams messages at click-time.

URLs shared in Teams chats are a common malware delivery vector. Safe Links provides time-of-click protection even for links shared days before detonation.

Defender portal → Email & collaboration → Policies & rules → Safe Links → [Policy] → Teams
2.1.5 L2 Safe Attachments for SharePoint, OneDrive, and Teams ▼

Enable Safe Attachments for SharePoint/OneDrive/Teams to scan files shared in channels and chats.

Files shared in Teams are stored in SharePoint/OneDrive. Without this control, malicious files can be shared and downloaded before any scanning occurs.

Defender portal → Email & collaboration → Policies & rules → Safe Attachments → Global settings → Turn on Defender for Office 365 for SharePoint, OneDrive, and Teams
2.4.4 L1 Zero-hour auto purge (ZAP) for Microsoft Teams is on ▼

Enable ZAP for Teams so that messages containing malicious content are retroactively removed from Teams after post-delivery detection.

ZAP for Teams extends the email ZAP capability to Teams messages — removing malware/phishing links after they've been delivered but before users click them.

Defender portal → Email & collaboration → Policies & rules → Anti-malware → [Policy] → Zero-hour auto purge settings → Enable ZAP for Teams User Impact: Low
3.2.2 L1 DLP policies are enabled for Microsoft Teams ▼

Ensure DLP policies include Teams as a workload, covering both channel messages and private chat.

Sensitive data (PII, financial data, credentials) is frequently shared in Teams. Without DLP coverage, Teams becomes a blind spot for data leakage.

Purview portal → Data loss prevention → Policies → [Policy] → Locations → Teams chat and channel messages → On User Impact: Low
