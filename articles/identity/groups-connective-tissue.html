Groups:The Connective Tissue

Everything in this series — authentication methods, passkeys, CA policy, guest access — is enforced through group membership. Get groups wrong and the rest of your identity work fails quietly in the background.

Entra Identity June 9, 2026 10 min read
Continue Reading

We've covered why identity is the perimeter, how authentication methods differ, why passkeys finally solve the friction problem, and how guest identities introduce trust problems most tenants haven't thought through. All of that work — every policy, every control, every phishing-resistant credential — gets enforced through group membership. Groups are where abstract identity strategy becomes concrete access control.

Get groups right and the rest of your identity posture scales. Get them wrong and you spend the next three years fighting access gaps, stale memberships, CA exclusions that nobody can explain, and a slowly deepening dependency on an AD environment you were supposed to be migrating away from.

"Identity lives and dies by groups. Every MFA policy, every CA exclusion, every app assignment, every privileged role — it's all groups underneath. The cleaner your groups, the more your security controls actually work."
01 Why Groups Are the Control Plane

Look at everything covered in this series through the lens of group membership and a pattern emerges.

🔑
Authentication Method Targeting When you enable passkeys or restrict SMS OTP in the unified Authentication Methods Policy, you scope it to groups. The rollout is only as clean as the group membership.
📋
Conditional Access Policies Every CA policy has an include and an exclude. Both are groups. The exclude group is where exceptions live — and where ungoverned groups create the gaps attackers look for.
👤
Passkey Profiles Device-bound for admins, synced for standard users. That segmentation is enforced through group-based passkey profile targeting. No clean groups, no clean segmentation.
🤝
Guest Access Scoping What a guest can reach inside your tenant is determined by the groups they're added to. Stale group membership is stale access. Access review scope is a group.
🛡️
PIM Role Assignments Privileged Identity Management activates roles through group membership. The security of your admin tier is only as strong as the integrity of those groups.

Groups aren't a supporting character in this story. They're the mechanism through which every control in this series actually functions. A group with stale membership silently undermines every policy that targets it.

02 The AD Dependency Problem

Most organizations in the SMB and MSP space sit somewhere on a spectrum between "still fully on-prem AD" and "genuinely cloud-native." Very few are at either extreme. The majority are in a hybrid middle ground that they've been trying to move out of for years without a clear path.

The honest reason hybrid stays sticky isn't technology. It's group dependency. Every time a new app gets provisioned with an AD security group, every time a new printer gets configured with a domain group, every time a new share gets NTFS permissions from an AD object — you're building a new dependency on the on-prem directory that makes leaving harder. Not impossible. Just harder, and more expensive to unwind later.

The Hidden Cost of AD Group Sprawl ⚠️

Every AD group with no Entra equivalent is a dependency that ties at least one workflow to on-prem infrastructure. Over time these accumulate quietly: a shared drive here, a legacy app there, a printer group that nobody can touch. The cost isn't visible until you try to decommission a domain controller and realize how many things still depend on it. The time to audit is now, not when you're forced to.

03 The Groups-First Migration Strategy

The path out of hybrid dependency isn't a single migration event. It's a gradual transfer of group authority from AD to Entra, one persona and one workflow at a time. The principle is simple: stop building new things that depend on AD groups, and start moving existing workflows to Entra groups as the opportunity arises.

The key is identifying personas — the logical groupings of users by what they need access to — and building those as clean Entra groups from the start. Not AD groups written back. Entra-native groups with Entra-native membership management.

04 The Hybrid Bridge — Group Writeback v2

For organizations that still have legitimate on-prem dependencies, Group Writeback v2 via Entra Cloud Sync is the bridge that makes an AD-minimized design possible without going cold turkey. It allows Entra-managed groups to be written back into AD, which means you can manage group membership in Entra while still satisfying the NTFS permissions and legacy app requirements that haven't migrated yet.

The key word is bridge. Writeback isn't a permanent architecture. It's a transitional tool that lets you establish Entra as the control plane for group membership today, while the on-prem dependencies are still being wound down. The goal is to shrink the list of things that need writeback, not to make writeback permanent.

Source of Authority Transfer 🔄

Group Source of Authority (SOA) in Entra lets legacy AD groups transition to cloud ownership without breaking workflows that still depend on them. Combined with the recently GA'd ability to convert individual users from on-premises authority to cloud-managed, this gives you the tooling to transfer control systematically rather than in a single high-risk cutover. Every group you move to cloud SOA is a group that no longer requires a functioning AD environment to manage.

05 Group Hygiene — The Part Most Orgs Skip

Clean group strategy and clean group hygiene are different problems. Strategy is about what groups you create and where. Hygiene is about whether those groups stay accurate over time. Most organizations have a reasonable strategy and almost no hygiene. The result is groups that were accurate when created and are increasingly wrong as time passes.

1
Every Group Needs an Owner

Mystery groups — groups with no assigned owner, no documentation, no clear purpose — are where your access review process breaks down. If nobody owns a group, nobody is responsible for its accuracy. Assign ownership at creation and enforce it. No owner, no group should exist.

2
Use Dynamic Membership Where the Logic Is Stable

If the rule for group membership is predictable — department equals Finance, job title contains Manager, assigned license includes Intune — dynamic groups are more reliable than manually maintained groups because the membership stays accurate without human intervention. The logic is the source of truth, not someone's last bulk import.

3
Run Access Reviews on Security-Sensitive Groups

Passkey profile groups, CA exclusion groups, PIM role assignment groups — these are the groups where stale membership has direct security consequences. Scope access reviews to them specifically. Require the group owner to confirm membership periodically. Auto-revoke on no response. The same access review mechanism from the guest post applies here.

4
CA Exclusion Groups Deserve Special Attention

The exclusion list in a Conditional Access policy is where the attack surface lives. Every account in a CA exclusion group bypasses whatever that policy enforces. These groups should have the smallest possible membership, clear justification for every member, regular review cycles, and ideally PIM-scoped access to be added to them at all. The CA Foundations series covers this in detail — this is the handoff point.
